      What do these mean?
      Played / Pure Maia / Shift
      Played is what the opponent actually did. Pure Maia is what plain, unstyled Maia would have done on average in the same positions. Shift is the difference: green means more than plain Maia, red means less.
      Captures, Checks, Castling
      Share of its moves that captured a piece, gave check, or castled.
      Leaves piece en prise
      Share of moves that left the moved piece attacked and not safely defended: a loose or hanging piece.
      King-zone pressure
      Average change in how many squares around your king it attacks, from −1 to +1. Positive means its moves aim at your king.
      Forward moves
      How far its pieces move toward your side on average, from −1 (retreating) to +1 (advancing).
      Style cost (KL)
      How far the playstyle pulled it away from plain human-like play. 0 means no pull (Balanced is always 0). Below about 0.1 is a light nudge; above about 0.4 is a strong pull. Technically: the average KL divergence, in nats, between the styled and plain move probabilities.
      Avg. human probability
      On average, how often players at that rating would have chosen the moves it played. Higher means more typical; chess has many reasonable moves, so 20–40% is normal.

      How StyleMate works

      StyleMate is a chess opponent that plays like a person at the rating you pick, not like an engine. It runs Maia-3, a neural network trained on millions of human games to predict the move a player at a given rating would actually make, mistakes included. Everything runs in your browser: the model (about 20 MB) downloads once and is cached, and there is no server or account.

      Opponent settings

      Model
      Maia-3 is the human-like opponent. Placeholder bot is a simple rule-based bot kept for testing; it is not human-like.
      Opponent rating
      The rating Maia imitates (600–2600, Lichess blitz scale). A 1200 opponent plays the kinds of moves, and makes the kinds of mistakes, typical of 1200 players. It is the rating it imitates, not a measured strength.
      Your rating
      Maia is also told who it is playing against, because people play a little differently against stronger or weaker opponents. It also feeds the eval bar. It doesn't change your side of the game.
      Playstyle
      Balanced is plain Maia: the moves real players at that rating choose, in the same proportions.
      Aggressive leans toward checks, captures, pushing forward and attacking the king, and is more willing to leave pieces loose.
      Solid leans toward castling and safe squares, and avoids leaving pieces hanging.
      Style strength
      How hard the style pulls (0–100%). At 0% it's plain Maia; higher values make the style more noticeable. Maia still only picks from moves a human at that rating might plausibly play; the style just changes which of those it prefers. Not used with Balanced.
      You play / Clock
      Choose White, Black or Random, and a time control or no clock. Take-back is off in timed games. Maia wasn't trained on clock times, so it doesn't speed up or panic in time trouble.

      Opponent settings start a new game when changed, so a game record always matches one setup. Display settings (eval bar, sounds) apply immediately.

      What the panels show

      Eval bar
      Maia's predicted score for White, for players at these two ratings: a win chance, not an engine evaluation. A big rating gap moves it a lot even in an equal position. Hover or tap the bar for details.
      Opponent's last decision
      The moves Maia considered and how often players at that rating would choose each one. The highlighted row is the one it played. It picks at random according to these odds, which is why it doesn't always play its top choice.
      Style report
      How the opponent's moves differ from plain Maia's in the same positions: captures, checks, king pressure, forward moves, loose pieces and castling. Style cost (KL divergence) measures how far the style pulled it from typical human play: 0 means no pull, under about 0.1 is a light nudge, over about 0.4 is a strong pull. Avg. human probability is how often players at that rating would have chosen its moves.
      Export
      Download or copy the game as PGN to analyze it on Lichess or Chess.com. The decision log (JSONL) records every opponent move with its probabilities, for anyone who wants to analyze the data.

      Controls

      Click or drag pieces. While dragging, right-click or press Esc to cancel the move. Flip board turns the board around without changing sides.

      Arrows and highlights (mouse): right-drag between squares to draw an arrow, or right-click a square to circle it. Hold Shift for red, Ctrl or Alt for blue, or both for yellow; plain is green. Drawing the same one again removes it; a left click or the next move clears them all.

      Limits

      The style weights are hand-tuned starting points, not learned from real players. The rating slider sets what Maia imitates; it is not a calibrated playing strength.
